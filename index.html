THE 48-HOUR TIMELINE

The Sprint Architecture

Every hour is engineered for rapid prototyping, mentorship milestones, and structured progress evaluation.

15:00 — 16:30

Participant Check-In & Team Kit Distribution

Badge pickup, workstation allocation in BRACU CSE laboratories, and network access setup.

17:00 — 18:00

Opening Ceremony & Keynote Address

Welcome remarks by BUCC executive committee, faculty advisors, and track challenge briefing.

18:00 SHARP

Hacking Sprint Officially Begins

Countdown clock initiates. 48 continuous building hours start across all 4 innovation tracks.

20:00 — 21:30

Technical Masterclass #1: Fast Prototyping Architectures

Optional technical session on rapid backend scaffolding, API integrations, and edge deployment.

23:00 — 00:00

Midnight Architecture Checkpoint

Teams register repository links and submit initial architectural milestone manifests.

09:00 — 10:30

Morning Sync & Developer Fuel

Breakfast service and informal track progress check with student ambassadors.

11:00 — 14:00

Round 1: Dedicated Mentor Clinic

Industry leaders, senior software engineers, and BUCC alumni conduct 1-on-1 team code reviews.

16:00 — 17:30

Technical Masterclass #2: Pitching & High-Impact Demos

Guidelines on crafting compelling 3-minute technical presentations and live demo failure resilience.

21:00 — 23:00

Round 2: Mid-Point Technical Evaluation

Verification of working codebases and progress milestones prior to the final overnight sprint.

12:00 — 14:00

Code Freeze & Final Repository Submission

All commit pushes finalized, README submissions, video walkthroughs, and deployment URLs locked.

14:30 — 17:00

Live Project Demonstrations & Expo

Top qualifying teams present live working prototypes before the distinguished judging panel.

17:30 — 18:30

Closing Fireside Chat: The Future of Computing in BD

Keynote conversation featuring prominent tech entrepreneurs and distinguished BRACU faculty.

19:00 — 20:30

Grand Awards Ceremony & Prize Distribution

Announcement of Champion, Runners-Up, Track Winners, and closing photo ceremony.

EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

How Projects Are Judged

Transparent, multi-dimensional assessment metrics focused on authentic engineering and practical viability.

30%

Technical Excellence

Complexity, architectural soundness, code quality, test coverage, and elegant handling of real-time or edge states.

25%

Innovation & Originality

Uniqueness of the approach. Novel synthesis of existing tools or pioneering new paradigms rather than replicating tutorials.

25%

Real-World Impact

Clarity of the problem statement and the measurable value the solution delivers to its target community or industry.

20%

Design & Polish

User experience, typography, accessibility, responsive execution, and resilience of the live demonstration.

COMMON INQUIRIES

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about team formations, logistics, rules, and hardware support.

BUCC NEXUS is a proposed 48-hour student innovation hackathon conceptualized by the BRAC University Computer Club (BUCC), the largest and most active student computing organization at BRAC University. It brings together undergraduate developers, designers, and problem solvers to build functional prototypes across four specialized tracks.

Yes! Interdisciplinary teams are highly encouraged. Students from Electrical & Electronic Engineering (EEE), Architecture, Mathematics, Business, and Biotechnology bring vital perspectives in hardware design, interface usability, and business viability.

Teams must consist of 2 to 4 currently enrolled undergraduate BRAC University students. Individual solo applications will be placed into a team-formation mixer session prior to kickoff.

No. All core application logic, database schemas, and hardware integrations must be authored during the official 48-hour hackathon window. You may utilize open-source libraries, publicly available APIs, pre-trained AI foundation models, and standard UI component kits (e.g. Tailwind, Radix).

Teams are encouraged to bring their own microcontrollers (e.g., Arduino, ESP32, Raspberry Pi) and sensor modules. BUCC will maintain a backup hardware depot with common breadboards, jumpers, sensors, and power supplies for loan during the sprint.

Priority interest registration is completely free. Any nominal fee for lab security and swag kit subsidization will be announced upon official university approval.
